Critical Response: Motherhood in Crisis

Introduction

The graphic novel series "Motherhood in Crisis" shows the dangerous situation for pregnant women in Sierra Leone. In this country, one out of every ten women dies while giving birth. The stories show that the healthcare system is very weak because of past diseases like Ebola and COVID-19. The main problem is that women have to travel a long way to find a hospital, which often leads to tragedy.

Summaries of the Stories

  • Memunatu’s Story: Memunatu lives in a small village far from any doctor. She had to stay home to give birth with a helper who did not have real medical training. After being in labor for two days, she suffered a serious internal injury. This caused her physical pain and made her feel ashamed and alone for a long time.

  • Heela’s Story: Heela was a student with big goals. However, when she became pregnant, she was forced to stop going to school. Her story shows how pregnancy can "steal" a young girl's future because she loses the chance to finish her education.

  • Kadiatu and Tetteh’s Stories: Kadiatu’s story is about the sadness of losing a baby because the clinics do not have the right medicine or equipment. Tetteh’s story shows the even harder struggle of being pregnant while in prison, where there is almost no medical help for mothers.

Conclusion

In conclusion, these stories show that the high death rate for mothers is caused by poverty and a lack of hospitals. Many women have to rely on untrained helpers because the real doctors are too far away. By telling these four personal stories, the graphic novel helps us understand that Sierra Leone needs better healthcare so that having a baby is not a life-threatening risk.
